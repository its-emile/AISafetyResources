    DeepMind cofounder's examination of transformative technology risks and containment strategies

    BookIntroSep 2023

    About this book + curator's take

    A general-audience book by DeepMind's co-founder on why AI and synthetic biology are hard to contain, and the mix of technical, corporate and government measures he thinks containment would take.

    Curator's take: Suleyman argues that mitigating this transformation is urgent because it is inevitable, with a specific warning: AI, synthetic biology and quantum computing are rising at the same time as cross-pollinating, general-purpose revolutions. He states the risks plainly, from accidental catastrophes to malicious actors.

  • Nick Bostrom: Superintelligence

    The philosophical groundwork for today's debates on control and power

    BookIntro2014

    About this book + curator's take

    Bostrom's philosophical study of what happens if machines surpass human intelligence, and how such systems might be controlled.

    Curator's take: Too old to say anything tactical or technical about LLMs, but its questions on concentration of power, AI control and convergent instrumental goals are still central more than a decade later, through ASL-3 model releases and agent threats. Its logic runs straight into Yudkowsky and Soares's book, though critics have questioned how inevitable those dynamics really are.

    Similar resources

    Stuart Russell: Human Compatible · Mustafa Suleyman: The Coming Wave - unprecedented risks of AI & adjacent tech + how we might contain them · Max Bennett: A Brief History of Intelligence

    The strongest-form argument that superintelligent AI would end humanity

    BookIntroSep 2025

    About this book + curator's take

    Yudkowsky and Soares argue that building superhuman AI with current methods would almost certainly be fatal, and illustrate it with a fictional power-seeking system called Sable.

    Curator's take: If the title doesn't spell out inevitability, the compressed timelines and the cold, almost alien calculation of its power-seeking antagonist, Sable, certainly do. Each argument is poignant.
